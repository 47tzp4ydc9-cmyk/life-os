# GLW (Corning) thesis

## Summary

Corning is the large-cap, stable, dividend-paying anchor for the optical/photonics theme. Direct beneficiary of AI data center buildout via optical fiber and connector demand. Less torque than AAOI/LITE/COHR but durable cash flow and dividend cushion.

## Why this works

- Hyperscaler AI buildout requires massive amounts of fiber + connectors — Corning is a default supplier with decades of optical glass leadership.
- Premium-selling around it works (the Aug 175P closed +$950 on Jun 24) — IV pops on tech selloffs let cash-secured puts get in at lower effective cost.
- Anchor position role: stable enough to size up, not so boring that returns are dead.

## What kills it

- Datacom segment revenue stalls for 2 consecutive quarters — implies AI optical demand is plateauing or a major customer shifted suppliers.
- A major hyperscaler signals an AI capex pause — would deflate the whole optical cohort, GLW included.
- ASP compression on optical connectors from new entrants — margin story breaks.

## What I'm tracking

- Quarterly datacom revenue and segment margin.
- Hyperscaler capex commentary (MSFT, GOOGL, META, AMZN).
- Optical connector pricing trends — proxy for competitive intensity.
- Entry status — wanted 200 shares around $210-215; stock ran to $223+ on Jun 25, may need to wait for pullback or sell a CSP at $210-215 strike.
- **Index/ETF mechanical flow events** (Russell reconstitution, S&P additions, sector ETF rebalances) — added 2026-06-30 after the Russell move; these can move price 10%+ independent of fundamentals and were not previously on the radar.

## Learnings

### 2026-06-30 — Russell reconstitution drove a +20%+ move we didn't see coming

**What happened:** GLW closed $220.28 on Jun 26. FTSE Russell's semi-annual reconstitution became
effective at the 9:30 AM ET market open on Jun 29 — Corning was reclassified out of several value
benchmarks and added to the Russell 3000 Growth, Russell 1000 Growth, and Russell Top 200 Growth
indexes. The stock whipsawed down to $218.44 in the first ~30 min (likely some initial sell pressure
from value-index funds dropping it), then violently reversed as growth-index funds had to buy:
$218.44 low → $246.09 by 11:00 AM ET (+12.7% in ~90 min) → $255.71 by close (+16% on the day) →
continued to $265+ intraday on Jun 30.

**Timeline of public information:**
- May 22, 2026, after 6 PM ET — FTSE Russell posts preliminary reconstitution membership list
  (first public signal GLW could shift indexes). This was knowable ~5 weeks in advance.
- Jun 8, 2026 — reconstitution membership locked/finalized ("lock-down" period).
- Jun 29, 2026, 9:30 AM ET — reconstitution becomes effective; mechanical fund flows hit the tape.

**Price data (ET):**

**What we missed:**
1. **No catalyst calendar tracking for mechanical index events.** `related_catalysts` was empty
   in this thesis until today. Russell reconstitution dates are published months in advance and
   are 100% knowable — this should have been logged as a catalyst the moment the preliminary list
   came out May 22, especially given GLW's run-up made a growth-index reclassification plausible.
2. **Treated GLW as a "stable anchor" without re-checking that framing as the stock re-rated.**
   The thesis was written assuming low volatility / dividend-anchor behavior. By the time of the
   Russell event the stock was already up significantly off its base, trading at a much higher
   multiple, and behaving more like a momentum/growth name than the "boring compounder" the
   thesis described — the framing should have been revisited before the event, not after.
3. **Entry was never adjusted after price moved away.** Target entry was $210-215; thesis noted
   on Jun 27 that price had already run past that to $223+, but no decision was made to either
   chase, wait, or abandon the entry — it just sat unaddressed while price kept extending.

**What to do differently:**
- When opening or maintaining a thesis on any stock with conviction ≥7, check whether it's a
  current or likely-future Russell (or S&P, Nasdaq-100, etc.) reconstitution candidate, especially
  for names that have had a large 12-month run (style drift from value→growth is exactly what
  happens to former "value" names that re-rate higher). Log the date as a catalyst the moment
  preliminary lists are public.
- Index-flow-driven moves are NOT validation of the fundamental thesis. Don't update conviction
  upward off price action that's explainable by mechanical fund flows — wait for the next
  fundamentals data point (earnings, hyperscaler commentary) before re-rating conviction.
- When an entry target is missed because price runs away, make an explicit decision (chase /
  wait for pullback / abandon) rather than letting the thesis go stale on entry status.
